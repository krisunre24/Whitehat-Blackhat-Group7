Assignment 2: White Hat/Black Hat Visualization

Kiran Manoj & Trenton Regis

About This Assignment

Charts can show very different interpretations from the exact same numbers. This project visualizes one dataset twice: a black hat version that uses design choices to mislead the viewer, and a white hat version that presents the data clearly and honestly.

Choose a visualization below to explore it.

Black Hat Visualization

This visualization is intentionally inappropriate for the audience. The axes are scaled with the goal of misleading the user.

White Hat Visualization

The visualization is easy to interpret for the intended audience. It is focused on clear communication, transparancy, and accuracy.

The Data

Both visualizations use annual greenhouse gas emissions from the OECD Data Explorer (Air Emissions: Greenhouse Gas Emissions Inventories).

Measure Total emissions excluding LULUCF
Unit Tonnes of CO₂-equivalent
Time span shown 1990 – 2022
Regions explored OECD, United States, OECD America

Key Distinctions Between Visualizations

The two versions present the same trend, but in very different manners. There is a major difference in the y-axis range, the title, and how large the change in emissions looks. Hover over the lines in either visualization to see exact values.

AI Usage

Claude was used to aid with formatting the index page and debugging. It was also used to adjust the scale of the White Hat visualization to make the visualization more interpretable to the user. It was also used to aid with interpreting how data aggregations could potentially introduce bias in the White Hat visualization. Additionally, Claude was used to help brainstorm ideas and get feedback on the direction of the Black Hat visualization. It was also used to help debug issues by breaking problems into smaller steps and identifying possible causes. It was mainly used to check whether choices such as the scale, endpoint emphasis, and region selection were having the intended effect.

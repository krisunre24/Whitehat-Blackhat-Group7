Disclaimers

  • The y-axis does not start at zero. It begins at -- to make changes over time easier to see.
  • The y-axis scale adjusts to each region you select, so steepness should not be compared across regions. Check the axis values.
  • The data is filtered to the years 1990 through 2022 to ensure there are no gaps in the data.
  • Possible bias: The values are total emissions, not per person, so larger or more populous regions naturally appear higher. They exclude land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF), which can add or remove significant emissions. The figures are self-reported national inventories, so reporting methods may differ by country. The regions also overlap (the United States is part of OECD America, which is part of the OECD), and each is an aggregate that can hide differences between individual countries. Lastly, three regions are shown, so this view does not represent every country.

White Hat Visualization Write-Up

In the black hat visualization, the range of values was purposely expanded with the goal of deceiving the viewer; the change in CO2 levels was meant to appear neutral and stagnant as time passed. The y-axis ranged from 0 to 25 gigatonnes of CO₂-equivalent. Since the actual values only take up a smaller part of that range, the changes over time appeared to be much flatter than they really are.

However, change should be measured relative to a baseline in order to deliver a more accurate comparison of CO2 levels over time. In order to do this, the scale was tightened to exclude values far beyond the range of y values. Now, the y-axis range is more restricted to the actual data values, enabling a more just comparison of CO2 levels over time.

This visualization was created with the goal of maximizing effectiveness: how readily perceived the information is to the viewer. It takes Weber's Law into account, which is a mathematical way of expressing how large a difference must be in order for humans to notice it. The law relies on the fact that humans judge based on the relative, not absolute difference of values. The scale adjustment for this visualization also considers this phenomenon. The title was also changed to provide an objective perspective of the chart instead of introducing potential bias in the user's perception of the data.

Data Source

The data came from the OECD Data Explorer, Air Emissions: Greenhouse Gas Emissions Inventories. We used annual greenhouse gas emissions, specifically total emissions excluding LULUCF, reported in tonnes of CO₂-equivalent. The visualization was built using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and D3.js.
